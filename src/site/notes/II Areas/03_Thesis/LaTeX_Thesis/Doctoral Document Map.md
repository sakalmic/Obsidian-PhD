Scientific claims, formal approvals and manuscript completion are separate states. A polished PDF is not evidence that any of these has been completed.

## Document lifecycle

1. Define a working proposal and its evidence requirements.
2. Record an approval only with a dated decision and an identifiable authority.
3. Link results to data and analyses before using them in conclusions.
4. Produce presentation/PDF snapshots from the agreed scope and mark their date and status.
5. Retain previous snapshots as historical material; do not use their dates or status labels to override current records.

## Manuscript transition

The existing LaTeX manuscript uses an earlier, broader plasma-protection concept. Its source is retained for reuse, but its chapter structure and results promises are not the current baseline. The current outline is the migration target. Until a content revision and rebuild are completed, existing thesis PDFs remain historical working snapshots.

## Current evidence status

As reviewed on 7 September 2026, CL-01 through CL-06 are hypotheses and have no linked datasets or analyses. This describes the evidence register, not the existence or absence of historical raw data. The next useful deliverable is one traceable result from raw records to an analysis and a claim decision.
