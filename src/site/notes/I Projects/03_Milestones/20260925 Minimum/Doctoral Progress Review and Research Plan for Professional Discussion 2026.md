This study supports the professional discussion after the first two years of doctoral study. Its purpose is to assess the research completed so far and determine whether the proposed scope, methods and resources provide a credible route to a doctoral contribution. It is a research proposal for assessment by the supervisor and board, not a record of their approval. ## 3. What the existing experiments establish

### 3.1 Apparatus and tested domain

The APL paper describes stainless-steel spherical electrodes of 20 mm diameter, gaps of 5 and 10 mm in atmospheric air, a Technix SR 40 kV–5 kW DC source and a picosecond PERLA laser. The reported nominal pulse duration is 1.2 ps and the repetition rate is 1 kHz. The tested wavelengths are 1030, 515 and 257.5 nm. Voltage and current were recorded using a Tektronix MDO4054B oscilloscope, a Tektronix P6015A probe and a Pearson 1423 current monitor. The paper reports at least five repetitions per configuration [2]. These are descriptions of the historical experiment, not a certification of the equipment currently available for the next campaign.

### 3.2 Reported voltage and delay results

The maximum relative voltage reductions tabulated in APL range from approximately 18% to 31%. For the 515 nm, 10 mm configuration, the tabulated reference and laser-assisted voltages are 29.5 and 20.3 kV, respectively, corresponding to approximately 31%. These values are campaign observations; the minima must not be relabelled as a calibrated 50% breakdown-voltage curve or as an operating reliability guarantee [2].

The delay table contains values from 0.164 to 8.124 µs. Within the reported series, decreasing applied voltage is associated with longer delay. However, the delay is referenced to an electrical laser-trigger signal. It is not yet the calibrated interval between optical pulse arrival in the gap and electrical breakdown [2].

**The general claim that increasing laser irradiance always shortens delay is not adopted.** The table and narrative in the local APL material require reconciliation on this point. No irradiance ordering is used to infer an optimum, interpolate a monotonic response or calculate economic benefit. The three settings are retained as reported operating conditions pending verification of their calibration and assignment. This open issue does not invalidate the proposal to measure the response prospectively using a calibrated protocol.

### 3.3 Limits relevant to the next phase

The historical campaigns demonstrate laser-assisted breakdown and provide a useful voltage and timing domain for planning. They do not yet demonstrate a shot-resolved channel-state predictor, transfer across independently realigned sessions, rare-event reliability, de-embedded physical jitter or an economic optimum. The small repetition count is also inadequate for strong statements about the tails of the delay distribution.

There is no measured product-level evidence here for longer electrode life, avoided network outages, short-circuit interruption, reduced maintenance of industrial switchgear or replacement of SF₆. Such benefits will not enter the base-case TCO as achieved savings. The future economic contribution evaluates a bounded laboratory service using measured performance and explicit cost scenarios.

## 4. Physical foundations, literature and research gap

### 4.1 Breakdown and the laser-created channel

Streamer inception and propagation involve local electric-field enhancement, charge transport and ionisation. The reduced electric field E/N connects electrical forcing to gas number density, but a single reduced-field value does not describe the full geometry or discharge history. Streamer physics, diagnostics and simulation methods provide the physical basis for selecting measurements and interpreting model limits [3].

A laser-created channel may provide initial ionisation and may subsequently alter the gas state through deposited energy. The relative contributions of free-charge dynamics and a longer-lived density perturbation must be treated as hypotheses for the studied conditions. Bright emission is not itself an electron-density, gas-density or temperature measurement. A channel descriptor can be useful empirically without being a direct measurement of one of those physical quantities; the dissertation must state that distinction.

The proposed work therefore begins with one demonstrably repeatable descriptor of geometry or temporal optical response. It adds a calibrated density-sensitive diagnostic only if available apparatus and an identifiable forward model justify that claim. Full plasma chemistry is not a mandatory deliverable. Electrostatic calculation for the actual electrode geometry and a reduced physical interpretation are sufficient modelling starting points.

### 4.2 Stochastic response and measurement uncertainty

At a fixed nominal setting, breakdown may occur at different delays or not occur within the observation window. Analysis must retain attempted shots, successful events, delayed events and acquisition failures as distinct categories. A shot without breakdown by the end of the observation window is a right-censored delay observation. Survival methods provide a basis for representing incomplete event times [4]. They do not justify treating an acquisition failure as a physically observed non-event.

Timing offsets, bandwidth, threshold detection and between-session variation enter the inference. Measurement models and propagation of uncertainty follow the principles of the GUM and its Monte Carlo supplement [5,6]. The uncertainty budget must include shared terms and correlations. Subtracting an assumed instrument variance from an observed jitter variance is justified only under the relevant independence and calibration assumptions.

### 4.3 From prediction to a technical and economic decision

Predictive accuracy and economic usefulness are different outcomes. A descriptor that improves a statistical score may have little value if it does not change the admissible operating choice, or if its calibration and acquisition costs exceed the resulting saving. Conversely, a lower-energy setting may be more expensive per compliant operation if it causes more unsuccessful attempts or longer resetting time.

Life-cycle costing provides the basis for comparing acquisition, operation, replacement and residual value over a common period. Sensitivity analysis is necessary when utilisation and service life are uncertain [7]. In this dissertation, that method is adapted to a laboratory switching service; building-specific assumptions or foreign discount rates are not transferred automatically.

### 4.4 Bounded originality claim

The proposed contribution is the experimentally traceable connection between channel observables, stochastic breakdown outcomes and cost-constrained operating decisions in a bounded family of atmospheric-air configurations. One reference geometry and polarity form the primary condition; a second geometry or polarity may test robustness when the core evidence is secure. The project does not claim the first laser-triggered gap, first survival model or first TCO analysis. Its originality must be established against relevant combinations of these methods, not by the presence of any method alone.

Before the confirmatory campaign, the literature comparison will explicitly record medium, pressure, pulse regime, timing reference, channel observable, treatment of failed shots, independent validation and cost boundary. This targeted comparison will refine the novelty statement. The present review provides a justified research direction, not a completed proof of worldwide novelty.

## 6. Experimental design and metrology for Tier 1

### 6.1 Controlled scope

Use atmospheric air only. Record ambient pressure, temperature and humidity rather than assuming constant density. Begin with one reference electrode geometry, polarity and confirmed laser branch. Add one second geometry or polarity as a controlled robustness check only when the primary result is repeatable, the comparison is powered and it fits the frozen workload. An optical condition may be added only when it tests transfer rather than opening a new parameter sweep. Do not perform a full factorial sweep over all wavelengths, focus positions, voltages, polarities and pulse regimes.

The initial sequence comprises no-laser reference measurements, a small optical/voltage screening design and a reduced confirmatory matrix. A near-threshold region is informative for probability estimation; highly successful operating points may be added for the defined application requirement. The robustness check is subordinate to the primary atmospheric evidence and may be dropped at the June stop/go gate. Controls and safe working limits are set by the actual apparatus and laboratory owner.

### 6.2 Operational quantities

An attempted shot is a trigger request made in the declared ready state. Breakdown is identified by a predeclared electrical criterion based on voltage and/or current. Delay is the interval from optical arrival at the interaction region to that criterion. The observation window and any early-event exclusion are fixed before confirmation. A compliant operation must meet the prescribed timing and electrical conditions, not merely produce visible light.

Normalized voltage k = U/U50 uses a contemporaneous no-laser reference obtained by a defined procedure and observation time. U50 is an estimated quantity with uncertainty. Electrode conditioning, ramp or dwell protocol and environment must be reported. Historical minima are not substitutes for this reference.

Channel features must be extracted from a defined time interval before breakdown. A feature containing the breakdown flash would leak the outcome into the predictor. If a feature is available only after a fixed landmark time, the model must condition on survival to that landmark and report coverage of early events. Failed shots must have the same planned acquisition opportunity. Non-shot-resolved diagnostics may support an ensemble interpretation, but not a claim of individual-shot prediction.

### 6.3 Measurement chain

| Quantity | Method | Required check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applied voltage/current | Calibrated probes and common acquisition clock | Bandwidth, offset, saturation and marker sensitivity |
| Optical time zero | Fast optical pick-off with known path relationship to the gap | Relative channel delays and pulse-arrival uncertainty |
| Laser input | Energy and beam measurement in the active configuration | Distinguish setpoint, delivered pulse energy and electrical consumption |
| Channel descriptor | Synchronized optical signal or calibrated image | Spatial/time calibration, repeatability and absence of outcome leakage |
| Environment/electrodes | Ambient sensors and configuration/history log | Link records to session, block and attempted shot |
| Economic inputs | Equipment records, power/time measurements and service information | Cost date, allocation rule, scope and uncertainty |

The measured delay is corrected by a calibrated net offset, with uncertainty propagated from optical path, acquisition channels, detector response and the event-marker algorithm. Pulse-to-pulse fluctuation and uncertain fixed offsets are distinguished. Where the physical variation cannot be resolved, the report gives a bound rather than claiming a de-embedded jitter value.

### 6.4 Sample size and independent repetition

The pilot determines event rates, between-session variability and the feasible number of conditions. Confirmatory sample size follows a declared interval-width or power target, with session clustering included. There is no universal five-shot or hundred-shot requirement. For perspective, even 100 independent successes without a failure imply only about a 0.9705 one-sided 95% lower confidence bound on success probability; they do not establish 99.9% reliability. This follows from p_lower = 0.05^(1/n) under the independent binomial assumption.

Block by day and electrode history, randomize settings within safe blocks and repeat the principal comparisons in independently realigned sessions. Hold out complete sessions rather than randomly mixing nearby shots into training and validation. The final sample count, stopping rule and effect-size threshold are frozen after the pilot. Repeated inspection must not become an undeclared stopping rule.

## 7. Prediction, validation and decision rules

M0 uses normalized voltage and measured laser input, with declared environment and session terms where appropriate. M1 adds the predeclared channel descriptor using the same training and validation partitions. Compare models of proportionate complexity so that an apparent gain cannot be attributed simply to an unrestricted increase in flexibility.

For probability, use a binomial model and report calibration as well as a proper scoring rule such as the Brier score. For delay, use a survival or accelerated-failure-time model that retains right-censored records; assess prediction of a predeclared time quantile or survival probability. Report timing distributions conditional on the stated population. Jitter among successful events is not an unconditional description of all attempted shots.

Model selection and preprocessing use training data only. Independent validation data remain untouched until the analysis and choice rule are frozen. Uncertainty is assessed at the session level where the sampling structure requires it. Report performance by condition as well as a pooled score, with the operating domain and failed predictions visible.

There are two prediction times. A pre-trigger operating choice can use settings and information from previous observations. A channel descriptor measured after the current laser pulse may improve prediction of that shot's subsequent breakdown, but cannot retrospectively choose the energy of that pulse. Tier 1 therefore evaluates an offline operating rule using repeated data and calibrated configuration-level response. It does not claim real-time control of the same shot. Repeated online adaptation or a controller belongs to Tier 3.

Freeze the recommended setting using training/calibration data and the declared cost scenario. Test its technical feasibility on a separate session. Selecting the cheapest condition on the final validation results and reporting those same results as independent confirmation is prohibited. If the best choice changes materially across plausible cost scenarios, report the alternatives and the information needed to distinguish them.

### 7.1 Role and execution of the theoretical model

The theoretical work starts with an electrostatic field calculation for the measured electrode geometry and applied voltage. Record geometry, boundary conditions and numerical convergence, then use the resulting field distribution to interpret the tested operating domain. This is an input calculation, not a simulation proving that breakdown will occur.

Next, construct a reduced description of channel evolution using only states that the diagnostics can constrain. Compare plausible charge-loss and gas-density timescales with the observed timing; distinguish measured inputs, literature parameters and fitted parameters. An optical descriptor may remain an empirical proxy if conversion to a physical state is not identifiable. A full three-dimensional plasma-chemistry model is not required.

Connect the reduced description to the probability or survival model and compare it with M0 on the same independent sessions. A proposed mechanism must earn its place through identifiable parameters, sensitivity and predictive performance. Report what the data cannot distinguish. The modelling deliverables are a field map, an explicit reduced-model specification, a parameter/uncertainty table and an independent predictive comparison, rather than a promise to reconstruct every discharge mechanism.

For the economic operating choice, use the response available before selecting a setting. If M1 uses a channel feature measured after the laser pulse, integrate over that feature's configuration-dependent distribution before predicting the cost or compliant-operation probability of a future attempt. Charge recurring diagnostics only when the evaluated operating service uses them; document research and commissioning costs separately. This keeps shot-level prediction and configuration-level TCO consistent.

## 8. Mandatory techno-economic assessment

### 8.1 Decision, service and comparison boundary

The reference service is a scheduled, laser-assisted switching operation in the defined atmospheric-air laboratory gap, with a specified electrical test condition and allowed timing window. The decision is which validated laser/voltage configuration provides that service at the lowest cost. The initial comparison is among feasible configurations of the same architecture, not between unrelated laboratory gaps and industrial circuit breakers.

Define demand, timing interval, acceptable success probability, electrical duty and reset protocol with the supervisor and laboratory partner before cost ranking. Those numerical requirements are inputs to the study, not values inferred from whichever configuration performs best. Report both total cost for the required demand and cost per compliant operation. A setting that cannot satisfy demand or performance is infeasible even if its nominal cost per attempt is low.

### 8.2 Two ownership scenarios

**Shared facility:** allocate use of the existing laser and diagnostics by a documented access-price or resource-consumption rule. Do not charge the full acquisition cost and the facility access fee for the same service. Costs already incurred are distinguished from incremental decision costs.

**Dedicated installation:** estimate the investment and operation of a functionally specified standalone installation, using dated quotations or documented ranges where possible. This is a scenario until such an installation and its performance are established. Do not assume an inexpensive replacement laser will reproduce PERLA performance without evidence.

The two perspectives are reported separately. A low marginal cost at HiLASE is not proof of a commercially viable standalone device. Conversely, allocating the entire research laser to one experiment may overstate the cost of a shared service.

### 8.3 Cost model and data

For configuration x, horizon H and discount rate r, define discounted total cost as:

$$
TCO_x = C_{0,x} + \sum_{t=1}^{H}\frac{C_{op,x,t}+C_{rep,x,t}+C_{fail,x,t}}{(1+r)^t}-\frac{R_{x,H}}{(1+r)^H}
$$

C0 is initial investment within the selected boundary; Cop covers operating resources and scheduled maintenance; Crep covers replacements; Cfail covers incremental failure consequences not already included elsewhere; R is residual value. Use a consistent price year, currency and real or nominal discounting convention. Record VAT treatment according to the selected decision-maker. Depreciation is not added as a second cash expenditure to an already included acquisition cost.

| Input | Preferred evidence | Treatment when unavailable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laser, optics, diagnostics and installation | Dated records or quotations | Explicit investment range |
| Facility allocation and utilisation | Access terms, booked and productive time | Separate utilisation scenarios |
| Electricity and cooling | Measured system input in active/idle states | Documented equipment bounds; never optical energy alone |
| Operator and alignment time | Session time logs and labour-cost convention | Measured pilot distribution or labelled range |
| Maintenance and replacement | Service records and component history | Scenario range; no life-extension claim |
| Successful/timely operations | Independent experimental validation | Confidence intervals and unmet-constraint reporting |
| Reset after failure | Observed sequence, time and consumables | Explicit reset scenario without assumed automatic retries |

The optical pulse energy measures experimental input to the gap. Wall-plug electricity, standby consumption, cooling and setup time determine the corresponding operating cost. Their relationship is measured or bounded separately.

### 8.4 Performance-normalized cost and constraints

Let q(x) denote the probability of a compliant operation, including the specified timing requirement. With A(x) attempted operations over the horizon, expected compliant output is A(x)q(x) for a stationary scenario. A transparent diagnostic metric is:

$$
c_{ok}(x)=\frac{TCO_x}{A(x)q(x)}
$$

This ratio uses discounted costs and an undiscounted physical output count; that convention is stated explicitly. For fixed-demand comparison, A(x) and the associated time and operating cost must be adjusted consistently to the demand and capacity model. The ratio alone is not a substitute for satisfying demand. If repeated attempts are allowed, success, reset time, capacity and dependence between attempts are modelled explicitly. Failure cost includes only additional consequences, avoiding double counting energy, operator time or lost production.

The feasible domain requires the chosen lower confidence bound on q(x) to meet q_req, the timing requirement to hold, and the operating duty to remain within the validated apparatus limits. If no point qualifies, report no demonstrated feasible solution. Do not relax constraints after seeing the results to create a preferred operating point.

### 8.5 Uncertainty and economic contribution

Evaluate sensitivity to utilisation, investment allocation, diagnostic cost, electricity, labour, failure/reset time and replacement interval. Propagate measured performance uncertainty jointly with cost uncertainty while preserving known correlations. Use scenario ranges when a probability distribution lacks evidence. A Monte Carlo output does not make unsupported inputs empirical [6,7].

The required outputs are the feasible operating domain, cost ranking with uncertainty, break-even conditions and the inputs that most affect the decision. Add the incremental cost of diagnostics before claiming that M1 creates economic value. If the cost ranking is unstable, the result is the boundary between preferred choices and the value of better information, not a single precise payback period.

This contribution links physical performance to resource allocation and investment/operation choices relevant to K13116. It remains bounded to the service studied. Avoided grid outages, industrial asset-life extension, avoided SF₆ costs and field reliability are excluded from the base case because the required system evidence is outside Tier 1.

### 9.2 WP1 — Establish the no-laser reference (Jan–Feb 2027)

**What to establish:** how the selected atmospheric gap behaves without laser assistance.

1. Fix one reference geometry and polarity; document electrode history and ambient conditions.
2. Record both breakdown and no-breakdown attempts under a declared voltage/observation procedure, repeated across independent sessions.
3. Estimate the contemporary $U_{50}$ curve and uncertainty; define $k=U/U_{50}$ for subsequent comparisons.

**Output:** reproducible baseline and its limits. **Theory in parallel:** calculate the static field in the actual geometry and test sensitivity to geometry, voltage and numerical mesh.

### 9.3 WP2 — Test whether the laser channel is observable (Mar–Apr 2027)

**What to establish:** whether a pre-breakdown channel feature can be measured reliably for each relevant shot.

1. Measure actual pulse energy, beam/focus properties and optical arrival time in the confirmed laser configuration.
2. Run a small laser-only and combined laser/HV pilot with synchronised waveforms and one candidate optical descriptor.
3. Repeat after realignment; determine whether the descriptor is available before breakdown and is more than detector noise or the breakdown flash.

**Output:** an interpretable descriptor **or** a quantified diagnostic limit. **Theory in parallel:** specify a minimal channel-evolution model using only identifiable states; keep an optical proxy distinct from direct electron density.

### 9.4 WP3 — Choose the decisive test (May–Jun 2027)

**What to establish:** a compact confirmatory design that can answer the dissertation question with available time.

1. Screen a small set of measured voltage/laser conditions; block by session and electrode history.
2. Use pilot event rates and session variation to determine attempt counts, primary contrasts and a stopping rule.
3. Freeze outcome definitions, M0/M1 inputs, channel-feature window and whole-session validation split. Hold a June supervisor stop/go review.

**Output:** dated decision and frozen confirmation protocol.

### 9.5 WP4 — Acquire new confirmatory evidence (Jul–Oct 2027)

**What to establish:** probability and delay distributions, with uncertainty, for the selected atmospheric conditions.

1. Execute the frozen matrix; retain every ready-state attempt, including no-breakdown and premature events.
2. Link optical timing, voltage/current, laser input, channel descriptor, environment and electrode history by shot ID.
3. Repeat principal contrasts in a separately initiated/realigned session and reserve complete sessions for later validation.

**Output:** QC-passed new dataset and Paper 1 evidence, clearly separated from the seven May 2024 and three February 2025 historical series. **Theory in parallel:** fit a baseline probability/delay model on training data and compare measured channel timescales with the reduced physical description.

### 9.6 WP5 — Test prediction on unseen sessions (Nov 2027–Feb 2028)

**What to establish:** whether channel information improves prediction beyond voltage and measured laser input, and whether a chosen setting remains technically feasible.

1. Freeze M0 (settings/context), M1 (M0 plus a predeclared pre-breakdown channel feature), preprocessing and scoring before opening reserved sessions.
2. Compare predicted breakdown probability and time-to-event distributions with complete held-out sessions; retain censored attempts and show failure regions.
3. Select an operating point from training/calibration evidence, then test that **preselected** point independently. A feature measured after a pulse cannot retrospectively select that pulse's energy.

**Output:** validated prediction or a documented lack of improvement, plus an independent technical check.

### 9.7 WP6 — TCO synthesis (Mar–Apr 2028)

**What to establish:** whether any validated configuration meets a predefined laboratory switching requirement at a defensible cost.

1. Consolidate costs logged since WP0: equipment use, electricity, operator/reset time, servicing and replacement. Distinguish research diagnostics from diagnostics required for routine operation.
2. Combine independently validated performance from WP5 with these costs; compare shared-facility and dedicated-installation scenarios separately.
3. Report feasible choices, uncertainty, sensitivity and break-even boundaries, or no demonstrated feasible/stable choice. Prepare Paper 2.

**Output:** bounded TCO and a defensible operating-choice result.
