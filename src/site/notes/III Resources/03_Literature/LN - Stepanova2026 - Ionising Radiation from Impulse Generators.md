# Studium emise ionizujícího záření při vysokoenergetických atmosférických jevech v laboratorních podmínkách

## Bibliographic metadata

- **Author:** Dagmar Štěpánová
- **Type:** Doctoral dissertation
- **Institution:** CTU in Prague, Faculty of Nuclear Sciences and Physical Engineering, Department of Dosimetry and Application of Ionising Radiation
- **Academic year:** 2025/2026
- **Local source:** `III Resources/03_Literature/FEL/Stepanova_Ph_D.pdf`

## High-level synthesis

Eight experiments at two high-voltage impulse generators and one high-current impulse generator used passive detectors to characterise photon/electron and neutron components. The work is an unusually direct methodological bridge to the proposed LTSG campaign because it addresses impulse discharges, FEL infrastructure, spatial non-homogeneity and detector immunity to electromagnetic interference.

## Experimental logic

- High-voltage generators represented the voltage component of lightning-like phenomena; the high-current generator represented selected current components.
- Passive detectors were chosen partly because intense EMP can disturb or disable active electronics.
- The detector set included TLD variants, solid-state track detectors, bubble detectors, passive neutron rem/spectrometry approaches, activation foils, RPL detectors and radiochromic film depending on campaign.
- Long-duration monitoring was complemented by controlled experiments with declared numbers of shots and detector positions.

## Main findings

- Photon/electron dose was significantly elevated for all three investigated generators.
- At the FEL high-current generator, both photon/electron and neutron components showed statistically significant increases above background in the relevant experiments.
- For the tested atmospheric impulse, polarity did not produce a statistically demonstrated change in dose magnitude/distribution under otherwise comparable settings.
- A detailed international comparison mapped the field along the discharge and supported strong spatial non-homogeneity.
- Photon/electron signal most often occurred in the second half of the mapped discharge; a maximum significant absorbed dose of $1.2\pm0.4$ mGy was reported in the stated detector band/position.
- Thermal-neutron-sensitive response was significant in two height regions; the maximum reported gamma-equivalent neutron dose was $6.9\pm1.2$ mGy at the specified position.
- Thermal-neutron enhancement was not demonstrated for the tested switching impulse.
- Direct strikes of passive detectors during negative switching impulses suggested local absorbed dose near 200 mGy.

## Mechanism cautions

- Detection of a neutron-sensitive response does not by itself identify the production mechanism.
- Atmospheric photoneutron thresholds discussed include 10.55 MeV for $^{14}$N$(\gamma,n)$ and 15.7 MeV for $^{16}$O$(\gamma,n)$.
- Construction materials can introduce additional $(\gamma,n)$ channels at similar thresholds.
- Thermalisation, scattering and detector energy response mean that the measured field differs from the source spectrum.
- Laboratory lightning analogues reproduce only selected voltage or current components and cannot be directly scaled to natural lightning without a model and uncertainty statement.

## Transfer to the proposed LTSG dissertation

### Immediately transferable

- Four-state EMP/background controls.
- Passive detectors as a robust survey layer.
- Multiple detectors per position and explicit background sets.
- Spatial mapping along the entire discharge rather than one detector at one radius.
- Normalisation by shot count and electrical energy.
- Separate hypotheses for photon/electron and neutron components.

### New comparison enabled by laser triggering

At matched electrical conditions, compare:

- self-breakdown;
- conventional electrical triggering if available;
- laser-triggered breakdown.

The central question is whether laser preionisation changes only the trigger time/path or also changes high-field electron acceleration and the location/probability of radiation emission.

### Deferred work

Neutron experiments should begin only after a radiation survey, mechanism assessment, calibrated multi-response detector set and radiation-protection review. A null thermal-neutron result remains scientifically valuable.

## Open questions retained from the dissertation

- Is the apparent angular non-homogeneity reproducible with a geometry designed to test it?
- What is the radiation field for switching impulses using detectors sensitive beyond thermal neutrons?
- How do polarity effects differ between voltage-impulse and current-impulse systems?
- Can TLD neutron response be calibrated well enough for absorbed-dose inference in the mixed field?
