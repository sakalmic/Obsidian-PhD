# Generation of Electromagnetic and Particle Radiation Pulses in High Energy Density Plasma

## Bibliographic metadata

- **Author:** Jakub Cikhardt
- **Type:** Habilitation thesis in Applied Physics
- **Institution:** Czech Technical University in Prague, Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Department of Physics
- **Date:** 27 March 2026
- **Local source:** `III Resources/03_Literature/FEL/Habilitation_thesis_Cikhardt_2026.pdf`

## High-level synthesis

The thesis unifies two routes to high-energy-density plasma: high-power laser-target interaction and high-current pulsed discharge/Z-pinch systems. For the present dissertation, its main value is not to make the atmospheric LTSG an HED-plasma experiment, but to provide a rigorous diagnostic and interpretive framework for fast EMP, particle acceleration, bremsstrahlung and neutron measurements in electromagnetically hostile pulsed-power environments.

## Directly relevant findings

### EMP is a source-physics observable and an interference problem

- Broadband laser-driven EMP cannot be attributed only to vacuum-chamber eigenmodes.
- EMP bandwidth and spectral centroid changed with laser energy/intensity across multiple diagnostics.
- Multiple mechanisms can coexist: fast-electron escape, target charging and neutralisation current, time-dependent space charge, plasma-field dynamics and propagation resonances.
- Plastic targets produced stronger EMP than metal targets in one campaign; the highest reported field was 620 kV/m at 40 cm for a CH2 foil. This contradicts an overly simple return-current-only interpretation.
- Above 1 GHz, an isotropic-emission estimate gave peak power on the order of gigawatts; the assumptions make this an estimate, not a directly measured total power.

### Diagnostic lessons

- Use complementary D-dot/B-dot probes and broadband antennas.
- Preserve both time-domain and frequency-domain information.
- Document oscilloscope, cable, antenna and probe bandwidths; observed spectra are chain-limited.
- Separate near-field/conducted coupling from far-field radiation.
- Use passive/activation diagnostics where fast electronics can be corrupted by EMP.

### Particle and radiation interpretation

- Pre-ionised low-density foam enabled direct laser acceleration of electrons above 100 MeV and subsequent bremsstrahlung/neutron generation.
- In deuterium gas-puff Z-pinches, multi-MeV deuterons broadened neutron spectra beyond the nominal 2.45 MeV D-D value.
- Activation analysis reconstructed a 2-18 MeV spectrum and indicated that up to about 15% of measured neutron yield could arise from interactions of energetic deuterons with surrounding material rather than D-D fusion.
- Detector response, scattering and construction materials must therefore be included in source attribution.

## Relevance to LTSG work

1. Add EMP control measurements even when EMP is not the primary outcome.
2. Treat EMP spectrum as a potential fingerprint of channel/current dynamics only after de-embedding the environment.
3. Prefer fibre isolation and passive diagnostics near the discharge.
4. Record material and geometry changes because they may change both source and transfer function.
5. Do not infer a radiation mechanism from one detector channel.

## Limits of transfer

- The laser EMP experiments use intense laser-solid interaction in vacuum, not a modest-energy laser-triggered atmospheric gap.
- Z-pinch results involve much higher currents and plasma energy density.
- Quantitative amplitudes and particle energies are not transferable to the proposed LTSG without direct measurement.
- What transfers is the diagnostic logic, causal caution and multi-channel validation strategy.
